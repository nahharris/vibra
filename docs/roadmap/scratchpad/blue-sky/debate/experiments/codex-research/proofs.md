# Proofs, identity and the case for a language

[argued] Independent notes for Codex Round 1, covering A, B, D, E, J and M. I read the protocol and the blue-sky README, paradigms and relevant draft chapters 00, 01, 03, 04, 07 and spec README. I did not read Claude's review or debate artifacts. This is review evidence, not a Vibra conformance run.

## A and M: does a language earn its cost?

### Takeaway

[argued] The evidence supports machine-checked artifacts and restricted implementer authority more directly than it supports a new general-purpose language, mandatory content-store authoring, or twelve cell kinds. A language should remain a contestant against an agent harness around an existing verifier until an end-to-end comparison establishes an advantage.

### Cited Findings

- [cited] The vericoding benchmark includes different numbers of specifications in Dafny, Verus and Lean: 3,029, 2,334 and 7,141 respectively. Its headline success rates are 82%, 44% and 27%. These are observations over its benchmark, not a randomized syntax/type-system experiment. [Vericoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22908).
- [cited] AlgoVeri explicitly identifies non-comparable task distributions in earlier cross-language results, then aligns 77 algorithms across Dafny, Verus and Lean. It reports best correctness rates of 40.3%, 24.7% and 7.8% for those systems and identifies both automation and proof-construction barriers. [AlgoVeri](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09464v2).
- [cited] Clover's abstract describes consistency checks over code, docstrings and formal annotations, evaluated on textbook-level annotated Dafny programs. Its reported success therefore does not require inventing a new source language. [Clover](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.17807).
- [cited] The draft admits that none of its cited studies compares loops with recursion while holding the language fixed. [Draft open question 7](../../../spec/README.md#open-questions).

### Inferences

- [argued] **A1: the proposed cause is not isolated.** Lower Lean success does not establish that dependent types themselves cause lower success, nor that all dependent programming should be excluded. Tactic interface, library familiarity, models, problem distribution, and automation are separate variables. A one-page syntax task also cannot settle repository maintenance or proof productivity. **What changes my mind:** equal tasks, contracts, models, tools, token/latency budgets and hidden tests, with ablations for automation and notation. **Do instead:** compare a small Vibra core to a fixed Dafny subset plus a role-enforcing transactional harness, and to restricted Rust/Verus for tasks where resource control matters; choose the solution with fewer escaped defects per completed task at an agreed cost.
- [argued] **A2: author volume is not free.** Extra annotations, examples and redundant artifacts consume model context, verification latency and reviewer attention even when typing is cheap. Independent representations help only to the extent their errors are independent. **What changes my mind:** ablate mandatory annotation/duplicate specification requirements and measure hidden defects, repair cost and human review time. **Do instead:** require information at public trust boundaries, infer local facts, and request extra redundancy where a checker can actually compare independent evidence.
- [argued] **M1: cell count is architecture masquerading as surface simplicity.** `type`, `label`, `fn`, `lemma`, `interface`, `impl`, `port`, `model`, `proc`, `machine`, `notation`, `protocol` and `target` are thirteen distinct listed kinds in chapter 01. Uniform storage does not remove their different semantics. **What changes my mind:** implement the same small application under a reduced vocabulary and show concrete expressiveness or verification losses. **Do instead:** keep function/type declarations, assertions/examples as attached artifacts, explicit effect interfaces and one application form; defer interface laws, notation declarations and protocol cells until a workload requires them. Do not merge constructs merely because both can be represented as trees.
- [argued] **A3: the protocol's evidence ordering is too coarse.** A citation to an uncontrolled study does not establish a causal language-design conclusion, and a single-participant toy measurement does not outrank a direct logical counterexample. **What changes my mind:** evidence with a design, population and outcome relevant to the actual disputed claim. **Do instead:** retain the tags as provenance, while ranking relevance, internal validity, uncertainty and effect size; use logical counterexamples to refute universal statements regardless of their provenance tag.

### Gaps

- [argued] No controlled Vibra-versus-existing-verifier result was available in the documents reviewed. No conclusion about a training-corpus handicap being fatal follows; it is an unmeasured adoption risk.

## B, D and E: what the guarantees actually require

### Takeaway

[argued] Opaque claims and evidence provenance are defensible, but the current specification permits lower-grade evidence to stand where universal safety facts are required, and several strength, ownership and closure rules are incomplete. Those gaps must be resolved before calling admitted pure code total and unable to fail.

### Cited Findings

- [cited] Unison hashes a definition's syntax tree and stores human names separately. This supports content-based identity and rename stability, but does not by itself specify Vibra's separate claim/witness link semantics. [Unison](https://www.unison-lang.org/docs/the-big-idea/).
- [cited] The FP2 paper's author-institution abstract expressly conditions safe allocation-free in-place execution on arguments not being shared elsewhere. Its Koka implementation executes in place dynamically when possible. [FP2](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/fpsup2sup-fully-in-place-functional-programming/).
- [cited] Dafny distinguishes partial correctness when termination checking is disabled, and exposes fuel to bound recursive definition unfolding. These are explicit limits rather than automatic consequences of writing `decreases`. [Dafny reference](https://dafny.org/dafny/DafnyRef/DafnyRef).
- [cited] Verified but Vacuous distinguishes verifier acceptance, syntactic specification binding, and semantic fidelity to informal requirements. Its 99.1%/1.9% contrast concerns acceptance versus syntactic binding in a particular reproduced pipeline. It is not a universal estimate of how often all formal verification is meaningless. [Verified but Vacuous](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05590).
- [measured] I ran [proof-countermodels.ps1](proof-countermodels.ps1) in PowerShell and saved exact output in [proof-countermodels.json](proof-countermodels.json). It exhaustively checks finite mathematical countermodels, not a Vibra checker: a correct unit result has zero rejectable outputs; a two-result predicate is determinate for inputs 0 through 31 and indeterminate at 32; a deliberately wrong compiled candidate agrees with its reference at every sampled input 0 through 31 and disagrees at 1000.

### Inferences

- [argued] **B1: claim identity is not executable identity.** A caller hashes a callee claim, while execution needs a particular callee witness. The same caller cell can execute differently after a callee witness swap, especially when equivalence was only tested. Even with proved equal values, cost, resource use, evidence grades and available backend code can differ. Evidence attached only to caller cell id cannot describe all deployment closures. A frozen revision plus transitive witness selection needs to be part of executable/cache identity. **What changes my mind:** a two-witness deployment/replay test demonstrating immutable linkage, distinct execution manifests, and automatic recomputation of effective trust after swapping a dependency. **Do instead:** separate logical claim ids from executable closure manifests and key execution/results/build validation by the entire selected witness closure, evaluator semantics/version, drivers and policy.
- [argued] **B2: opacity does not imply fixed checking work.** A direct claim can mention another claim whose `means` mentions another; allowing unfolding creates transitive work. A 2,000-node direct closure does not bound that expansion, quantified search or solver effort. Nor does it bound the context an agent needs for cross-cell changes. **What changes my mind:** adversarial deeply nested `means` and cyclic claim examples with measured node/fuel/solver bounds and successful useful tasks within those limits. **Do instead:** use explicit unfolding fuel, proof-search quotas and summarized opaque contracts, counting the actual materialized obligation context; treat agent context as a separate measured budget.
- [argued] **B3: admission authority is not guaranteed by role names.** One principal may hold several roles; helpers may be created and claimed by the implementer; namespace resolution, policy enforcement, signatures and admission transactions protect the boundary. They belong in the security trust story, even if absent from the logical proof kernel. The kernel does not establish that a principal is entitled to approve an artifact. **What changes my mind:** negative tests for a witness-only principal changing aliases, helpers, policies, signing keys and target linkage to evade a fixed root claim. **Do instead:** define separate logical and security trusted bases, pin signed root claim trees and dependency manifests, and enforce authority in an authenticated transaction layer.
- [argued] **B4: nominal identity needs more than structural claim hashing.** The draft promises that two same-shape type declarations are distinct, but names are metadata and claim ids hash normalized claims. If declaration identity contributes no stable seed, two same-shape wrapped types can collapse to one claim id; the exact encoding is not specified enough to resolve this. Rejecting repeated normalized meanings also conflates sharing an implementation with forbidding independently meaningful declarations. **What changes my mind:** two same-shape nominal types remain unequal after hashing, renaming, copying and cross-store import, while intended aliases preserve equality. **Do instead:** specify an explicit immutable nominal identity seed and alias semantics; make duplicate-function detection a reuse suggestion rather than a universal admission ban.
- [argued] **D1: affine use is not uniqueness.** `let b = items` is legal under freely shared values. Consuming `items` at `own` need not consume alias `b`; mutating the buffer changes `b`, while copying abandons the unconditional constant-cost in-place guarantee. Ownership needs a formation rule, not only a use-after-consume check on a variable. **What changes my mind:** formal rules and conformance cases covering aliases through records, closures, nested arrays and shared slices, with allocation instrumentation. **Do instead:** either introduce an explicit unique/owned collection type with checked uniqueness and borrow rules, or use dynamic uniqueness/copy-on-write with honest worst-case cost. Keep affine resources (may drop) distinct from linear obligations (must consume).
- [argued] **D2: total mathematical functions can still exhaust a machine.** Termination does not bound steps or allocation usefully, unbounded integer multiplication is not constant physical work, and `diverge` debt explicitly admits functions without established termination. Unbounded recursion depth also means finite inputs can exceed stack unless all calls are tail calls. **What changes my mind:** budgets, allocation failure semantics and proof rules that cover every approved evaluator entry point, including claim evaluation. **Do instead:** distinguish logical totality from operational success; run evaluation with memory/step limits even for pure code, restrict debts in proof contexts, and describe resource exhaustion as a runtime outcome rather than promising its impossibility.
- [argued] **D3: lower-grade facts do not justify erased checks.** Chapter 04 permits tested/bounded evidence according to policy and says a called function stands for its claim. Suppose an index-producing helper promises an in-range result but violates that promise on an unsampled input. A caller can use that promise to justify `array.at` and fail at runtime. Effective grade propagation discloses the assumption; it does not make the memory access safe. **What changes my mind:** a type-safety argument covering admitted tested assumptions, plus an adversarial helper returning an out-of-range index. **Do instead:** require checked universal evidence for erased safety checks, retain dynamic checks for lower-grade safety obligations, or explicitly make the language's no-failure guarantee conditional on a closed proved/solved trust profile.
- [argued] **D4: the recursive-type criterion is not a complete well-foundedness rule.** Merely requiring one nonrecursive case does not address negative occurrences through function arguments or mutually recursive emptiness. A refined type also needs an inhabitedness story where generic operations assume values exist. **What changes my mind:** formal positivity/productivity rules and negative recursive-function-field tests. **Do instead:** start with strictly positive algebraic datatypes, explicit empty types and a separately checked recursion scheme; add richer type dependencies only when they remove a measured proof burden.
- [argued] **E1: the vacuity test rejects correct singleton codomains.** The draft requires some valid input/result pair to be rejected. A unit-valued total function with a correct reference meaning cannot reject any well-typed result. The finite countermodel records exactly one valid output and zero rejectable outputs. `returns any` exempts only determinacy, not vacuity, so it does not fix this case. **What changes my mind:** a rule that admits correct unit/singleton claims while rejecting genuinely unconstrained claims on larger domains. **Do instead:** check whether the specification adds information beyond typing and explicitly permit fully characterized singleton results; use configurable strength warnings where the property is legitimately vacuous.
- [argued] **E2: bounded strength checks cannot support universal determinacy.** The recorded predicate `n > 31 or r = 0`, with result domain `{0,1}`, has a unique result for every checked input 0..31 and two results at 32. This does not invalidate bounded checking; it invalidates reporting global determinacy from bounded evidence. Mutation scores are also relative to the witness, mutation operators, input domain and equivalent-mutant handling, so they cannot measure specification completeness independently. **What changes my mind:** an independently curated corpus with hidden under-specification defects, false-positive rates and explicit `unknown` outcomes. **Do instead:** report strength as separate bounded metrics, never as an unconditional property; require universal evidence only where a feature depends on uniqueness.
- [argued] **E3: equivalence and mutation are solver problems with real unknowns.** Arbitrary quantified claims, recursive functions and extensional equality cannot all be decided by an always-terminating checker. Testing a mutant does not establish non-equivalence; failing to prove equivalence does not establish it either. A concrete counterexample may be hard to construct even when an obligation is false. **What changes my mind:** a precise decidable fragment, proof format and behavior for timeout/unsupported formulas, demonstrated on the intended standard library. **Do instead:** make `proved`, `refuted`, `unknown`, `sampled` outcomes explicit per obligation and keep admission configurable without calling unknowns proof failures or silently trusting solver translations.
- [argued] **E4: the grade is not the whole evidence.** A bounded result over a tiny domain and a test over rare hostile cases are not ordered by semantic coverage merely because their grade names are ordered. Two stores may possess different evidence for the same cell, despite chapter 01 asserting identical evidence. **What changes my mind:** cross-store import/reproduction cases with different tools, seeds, assumptions and bounds, preserving all provenance without grade inflation. **Do instead:** represent evidence as immutable records keyed by obligation statement, assumptions, method/version, domain/bounds and artifacts, and derive policy verdicts from those records. Retain multiple evidence records instead of pretending evidence is a property solely of cell bytes.

### Gaps

- [argued] I did not run Vibra, Z3, FP2 or a Dafny implementation; the ownership and safety counterexamples are specification reasoning. The finite scripts demonstrate logical distinctions only. Formal counterexamples require the missing precise semantics to become conformance cases.

## J: does checking the core remove the compiler from trust?

### Takeaway

[argued] A type checker validates the proposed core program, not that a human-approved surface program was translated faithfully. Differential sampling similarly tests an artifact; it does not universally transfer source proofs to it. The draft should stop conflating these properties.

### Cited Findings

- [cited] Lean's validation guide explicitly separates proving a theorem from establishing what its statement means; it recommends stronger validation for potentially malicious AI-generated artifacts and describes dependency axioms. [Lean validation](https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/ValidatingProofs/).
- [cited] CompCert describes its compiler correctness claim as semantics preservation between source and generated code, explaining that source verification can otherwise be invalidated by miscompilation. [CompCert manual](https://compcert.org/man/manual001.html).
- [cited] Alive2's primary repository describes symbolic execution/refinement checking and explicitly excludes inter-procedural transformations from its supported validation scope. This is a materially more specific contract than sampled agreement. [Alive2](https://github.com/AliveToolkit/alive2).

### Inferences

- [argued] **J1: round trips can preserve the wrong interpretation.** A parser/printer pair can consistently map the surface word `add` to a subtraction tree and print it back as `add`. A round-trip test passes. Likewise name resolution can consistently choose the wrong same-typed claim id; the core still type-checks. A kernel checking only ids cannot detect intent substitution. **What changes my mind:** malicious parser, resolver and elaborator test doubles with translation certificates tied to a separately approved canonical claim artifact. **Do instead:** have the authority approve canonical resolved claim trees via an independent renderer, or trust a minimal parser/resolver; for untrusted elaboration require a checked source-to-core semantic relation rather than only core typing.
- [argued] **J2: sampled backend validation does not remove backend trust for universal claims.** The PowerShell countermodel agrees with reference `n + 1` on all 32 sampled inputs and returns `-1` at 1000 instead of `1001`. It models exactly why finite examples/generated inputs/journals are insufficient to justify all-input equivalence. **What changes my mind:** checked per-transformation proofs or a verified compiler for the actual subset, including procedure lowering and resource-metering semantics. **Do instead:** label compiled artifact assurance separately from source proof grade; initially trust the backend explicitly or ship an interpreter-only proved profile, then add certificate-checked lowering/optimization where feasible.
- [argued] **J3: the supposedly first-order core does not yet express the surface.** Surface functions are values; transforms accept them; nested named functions capture bindings. Core grammar allows only `call id` and no application of a function-valued term, closure allocation or explicit environment. `local` alone does not specify escaping closures. **What changes my mind:** complete lowering examples for a captured function passed through a list transform and returned as a value, with ownership/label preservation. **Do instead:** choose a small typed higher-order core, or specify closure conversion/defunctionalization and its validation; remove the unsupported first-order claim in the meantime.
- [argued] **J4: the trust boundary omits security and theorem translation.** Obligations generated from core are trusted, but a solver certificate validates the formula it is given. If the solver bridge mistranslates a claim, a checked solver proof can prove the wrong formula unless reconstruction connects it to the original obligation. Parser-independent signing, store role enforcement, primitive arithmetic, persistence recovery and driver confinement likewise need explicit assurances. **What changes my mind:** end-to-end proof checking from signed obligation tree through translated logic and certificate reconstruction, plus a component-by-component logical/security TCB inventory. **Do instead:** publish separate guarantees and trusted components for source meaning, proof validity, compiled execution, authority and durability; require a checked bridge for `proved`, keep an unverified bridge in the trusted base for `solved`, and treat tiny-kernel size as a measured engineering target.
